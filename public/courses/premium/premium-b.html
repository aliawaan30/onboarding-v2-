Load the Skill and Write the Brief

Your co-founder gets the premium builder, then the two of you decide exactly what this site is before a single image is made.

This is the part beginners skip and then wonder why the result looks generic. Ten minutes of deciding saves you a whole afternoon of regenerating things that were never going to work.

  2. 2

    Step 02Tell It Who You Are Building For.

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    Now hand it the business you picked on the last page. Swap the parts in square brackets for the real details.

    SAY THIS TO YOUR CO-FOUNDER
    Use your Premium Website Builder skill.

I am building a premium cinematic website as a demo for a real business. Here are the details:

Business name: [NAME]
What they do: [ONE LINE]
Where they are: [SUBURB, CITY]
What I want a visitor to actually do: [BOOK A TABLE / ENQUIRE / VISIT / BUY]
Their website now: [URL, OR SAY THEY HAVE NONE]
Their Instagram: [HANDLE, OR SAY THEY HAVE NONE]

Before we build anything, research them properly. Look at whatever is publicly visible: their site, their listing, their reviews, their socials. Then tell me what you found, what they are actually good at in their customers' own words, and what feeling their website should have. Ask me only for information you genuinely cannot find and that would change the design.
    SCROLL TO READ IT, OR JUST TAP COPY
    Answer its questions honestly, and say when you do not know. Never invent a review, a price, an award or an address for a real business. The skill is told not to, and you should not either. A demo with invented claims is the fastest way to lose a client in the first meeting.
    You should see: a real read on the business, not a generic summary. It should mention things only someone who looked would know.
  3. 3

    Step 03Get the Direction on Paper.

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    A direction is the one sentence the whole site obeys. Ember and Salt is unhurried, warm and tactile. Form Office is space, margin and material. Everything else follows from that.

    SAY THIS TO YOUR CO-FOUNDER
    Now write the direction for this site and show it to me before you build anything.

Give me all of this:

1. The governing idea in one sentence. What this site feels like.
2. The palette. Actual colours, and what they are drawn from in the real business.
3. Two typefaces and what each one is for.
4. How the images should look. Light, mood, materials, how close in we are.
5. The section map, top to bottom. For each section: what question the visitor is asking there, what content answers it, how it is laid out, and what image or film sits in it.

Keep it tight and specific. No generic words like modern, clean or sleek. Then stop and wait for me to say go.
    SCROLL TO READ IT, OR JUST TAP COPY

    Read it properly. If the direction sounds like it could be any business, tell it so and make it try again. This is the cheapest moment in the whole build to change your mind.

    That direction is too generic. It could describe any business in this industry. Go back to what is actually specific about [NAME], the thing a regular customer would mention, and write me a direction that could only belong to them.
    You should see: a direction you could read to the business owner and have them nod. Save it, you will refer back to it constantly.
  4. 4

    Step 04Get the Shot List.

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    Every image and every film you are about to make needs describing before you make it. This is the step that decides whether the media comes out usable.

    SAY THIS TO YOUR CO-FOUNDER
    Now write me the shot list, based on that direction.

For every image: what is in frame, where the subject sits, the light, the materials, the mood, and anything that must NOT appear.

For each film: the one camera move, what the subject does, what stays fixed in frame, and how the shot ends. One clear action per film, not a chain of different things.

Tell me which shots are films and which are stills, and which section each one belongs to.

Then, for each one, give me the finished prompt I can paste straight into a generator. Number them so I can work through them in order.
    SCROLL TO READ IT, OR JUST TAP COPY
    One move per film. A camera that glides past a building works. A camera that flies through a window, spins around a person, then cuts to a kitchen will fail, and you will pay for the failure. The skill knows this, but if it writes you a complicated shot, tell it to simplify.

    Keep these prompts somewhere you can copy from. A note, a doc, anywhere. You will be pasting them into Envato on the next page.

    You should see: a numbered list of prompts, each one ready to paste, with a film or still label and a section name.
  5. 5

    Step 05Ask It What It Still Needs From You.

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    The single biggest difference between a demo that gets you the client and one that does not is real content. Real services. Real prices if they publish them. Real opening hours.

    Before I go and generate the media, tell me exactly what real information about [NAME] you still need from me, and what you are currently guessing at. Be specific about anything you would otherwise have to invent. Then tell me the fastest way to find each one.

    Go and get whatever it asks for. Their menu, their service list, their hours. It takes ten minutes and it is the difference between a page that feels like theirs and a page full of filler.

    You should see: a short list of gaps. Fill the ones you can, and tell it plainly which ones you could not.
