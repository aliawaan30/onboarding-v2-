Get Paid

Two rules stop nearly all late payments, and there is a polite way to handle the ones that slip through.

  1. 1

    Step 01Send The Invoice

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    Two rules stop nearly every late payment.

    Ask before you hand over, not after.

    Name the day, not the number of days. Due in 7 days gets ignored. Due Friday gets paid.

    Your co-founder writes it, you send it, and the money lands in your account by bank transfer. Nothing else to set up.

    Write my invoice for [CLIENT NAME]: [WHAT I BUILT THEM] for [ADDRESS OR BUSINESS], $[AMOUNT]. Use my business details and bank details from memory. One page. Put a real due date on it, a day of the week, not in 7 days. Give it to me ready to send.
    You should see: a clean one page invoice with your details on it, ready to go.

    Around your fifth client it is worth turning on proper invoicing inside your bank, which numbers them and chases them for you. Your co-founder can fill in every field for you when you get there. Not needed today.

  2. 2

    Step 02Send The First Nudge When The Day Passes

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    Nothing dramatic. Most late payments are an inbox, not a decision.

    [CLIENT NAME] has not paid and the invoice was due [DAY]. Write me one message. Two lines. Friendly, zero pressure, assumes it just got missed rather than ignored. Include the amount and offer to resend the invoice. Do not apologise for asking. It is my money.
    You should see: a two line nudge that does not grovel.

    Read it out loud. If it sounds like you are sorry for existing, tell it to write it straighter.

  3. 3

    Step 03Ring Them On Day Seven

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    Two messages have gone unanswered. Do not send a third. A call from a local person they have met gets paid within the hour. A third text does not.

    [CLIENT NAME] still has not paid, a week past due, and two messages have gone unanswered. I am ringing them. Give me the opening line, plain spoken, under 20 words, and the one line I use if they say cash is tight right now. Do not make me sound annoyed and do not make me sound scared.
    You should see: two short spoken lines you would actually say.

    You could say the first one to a neighbour without flinching. If not, get it rewritten.

    Never threaten, go cold, or switch anything off without telling them. You are building a reputation in one small area, and people talk.

Paid, Or On The Way

Invoice out with a real day on it, and a nudge that does not grovel if the day goes past.
