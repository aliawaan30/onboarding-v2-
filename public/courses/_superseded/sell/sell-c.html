The number, then the price

You build the number in front of them out of their own answers. Then, and only then, you say what it costs.

  1. 1

    Step 01Understand why you build the number in front of them.

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    A number you work out in front of someone lands ten times harder than a number you bring with you. They watched it get built out of their own answers, so they cannot argue with it without arguing with themselves.

    Say the maths out loud as you do it. Right, so thirty calls a week, you reckon you miss about four in ten, that is twelve. Of those twelve, how many just ring the next bloke? Most of them. Say seven. And you would win most of those, so five jobs a week at four hundred and fifty.

    Use their numbers, then be conservative. If they say they miss about half, use forty percent. If they say a job is worth four or five hundred, use four hundred. The number will still be enormous, and every assumption in it is one they would defend.

  2. 2

    Step 02Run the maths for your lane.

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    Three prompts. Run the one for your lane and skip the rest.

    AI Callers:

    Work out what missed calls are costing this business a year. Here is what they just told me: Calls a week: [NUMBER] How many they reckon they miss: [NUMBER OR PERCENT] Of the missed ones, how many ring someone else instead of waiting: assume 60% Of those, how many would have become a paying job: assume 70% Average job value: $[NUMBER] Show me the maths line by line in plain numbers, then the weekly loss, then the yearly loss. Round down everywhere. Keep it short enough to read out loud in thirty seconds.

    AI Websites:

    Work out what having no proper website is costing this business. Here is what they told me: Enquiries a week now: [NUMBER] Where they come from: [WHAT THEY SAID] Average job value: $[NUMBER] What happens when someone looks them up at night: [WHAT THEY SAID] I cannot measure enquiries they never got, so do not pretend I can. Instead give me two honest things I can say out loud: 1. What one extra job a month would be worth to them in a year, using their own job value. 2. A plain sentence comparing that to what a website costs them once. No invented statistics. No lines about what percent of customers do anything. Just their own numbers.

    Property Walkthroughs:

    Work out what this agency is spending on walkthrough videos now, and what half looks like. Here is what they told me: Listings a month: [NUMBER] What they pay per walkthrough video now: $[NUMBER] Listings they skip the video on because of cost or turnaround: [NUMBER] Turnaround they get now: [DAYS] Give me: - what they spend a year at that rate - what they would spend a year at half that rate - the difference in dollars for the year - what the skipped listings would be worth at my price - one sentence I can say out loud that puts my price next to what they pay now Round down. Keep it to six lines.
    You should see: five or six plain lines ending in a number you can read out loud in under thirty seconds. If it gave you a spreadsheet, tell it: five lines, out loud, no table.
  3. 3

    Step 03Handle it when they do not know their own numbers.

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    This happens on about a third of calls. The owner genuinely has no idea how many calls they miss or what a job averages. Do not guess for them. Get them to guess.

    The owner does not know their numbers. Give me three questions that will get them to work it out themselves without feeling like they are being tested, for my lane. Things like what was the last job you booked worth, roughly, that get me to the same figures sideways. Then tell me what to do if they still cannot answer: what conservative number I should use instead, and the exact sentence I say so it is obviously an estimate and not me making things up.
    You should see: three sideways questions plus a fallback number and an honest sentence to say.

    The fallback sentence has to make it clear it is an estimate. Something like I am guessing here, tell me if I am way off. Anything that states a made up figure as fact is wrong. Make it redo it.

  4. 4

    Step 04Learn the pricing rule.

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    Price under the money left on the table. Whatever you charge has to be obviously smaller than what they lose or spend by doing nothing. That is the whole strategy. Everything below is just how it applies to your lane.

    AI Websites. $500 to $1,500 for the site. One price, said with a straight face. The sentence that sells it: it costs you about what one job pays you.

    Property Walkthroughs. $150 to $200 a video, and your pitch is half what they are paying now. You get that number from question three in discovery. If they pay $400 you charge $200.

    AI Callers. A setup fee of $1,000 to $3,000, then $500 a month. One monthly number for every client, whatever you built them.

    The setup fee moves and that is deliberate. A business missing five calls a month worth two grand is not paying $3,000 up front and you would be a mug to ask. A business missing fifty is getting a bargain at $3,000. Same product, different number, and the difference is what you learned in discovery.

  5. 5

    Step 05Know what it costs you before you quote.

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    Whatever you charge, some of it goes straight back out. Know these before you open your mouth.

    • Base44, about $50 a month. You, once, and it covers every client you ever have.
    • Call minutes if you are in the callers lane, cents a minute. You, covered by the monthly.
    • The phone number for a caller client, a few dollars a month. The client, in their own name.
    • Video credits if you are in the walkthroughs lane, per video. You, covered by your price.
    • The domain for a website client, fifteen to twenty dollars a year. The client, in their name.
  6. 6

    Step 06Get your quote recommended.

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    Based on what this business told me in discovery and the yearly number you just worked out, recommend my quote. Give me: - the setup fee, sized so it is obviously smaller than what they are losing or spending - the ongoing fee if my lane has one - one sentence on why that number and not higher or lower - one sentence I can say out loud that puts my price directly next to their number Use the pricing rules from this module for my lane. If a number you need is missing, say what is missing rather than guessing at it.
    You should see: two numbers, the reasoning, and one sentence you can say on the call.

    Check the setup fee sits comfortably under one month of their loss or spend. If it does not, either it misread the maths or you did.

You can price anything

The rule never changes: whatever you charge has to be obviously smaller than what doing nothing is costing them.
