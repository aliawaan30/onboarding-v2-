Why every business needs a website

Nothing to build yet. Watch the video, read this once, and get why this sells.

By the end of this module you'll have picked a real local business off your board and built them a full website, with their real services, reviews and phone number on it, finished and ready to show.

The truth of it

You Are Not Selling Websites

You're selling being findable and looking real.

Here's what happens to a business with no website.

A customer searches "plumber near me". Five results come up. Four have websites. One has nothing behind the name.

The customer doesn't ring to ask why. They just ring someone else.

That happens every week, and the business never finds out.

Why now

This Job Did Not Exist Two Years Ago

Building a proper website used to mean a designer, weeks of back and forth, and thousands of dollars. That is why so many small businesses still don't have one.

AI changed the cost and the time.

The same quality of site, the kind a designer would charge thousands for, now gets built in minutes, for a few cents of computer time.

The businesses still think it costs thousands and takes weeks. It doesn't any more. You are the only one in their life who knows that.

That gap is the whole business. It will not stay open forever, which is exactly why you are learning this now.

Why they say yes

They Already Know They Need One

You're not convincing anyone. They've been meaning to sort it for two years. You're removing the reason they haven't.

Because the normal way of getting a website is painful.

A web designer quotes thousands, takes six weeks, then asks them to write their own words and send photos.

Most businesses stall right there, and it dies.

You skip the whole painful bit.

Their name, services, reviews, hours and phone number are already public on their Google listing.

You build the site from that. They give you nothing.

Your leads

You Already Have the List

Most people starting a business have no idea who to sell to. You got past that in Module 3.

Your board is already full of businesses near you with no website at all. Their name, what they do, and their phone number, all sitting there waiting.

Every card on that board is someone who needs exactly what you sell, with their phone number printed next to their name.

You can top it up any time, for as many areas as you want, and it costs you nothing to run.

You are not hunting for customers. You are reading a list of them.

Your unfair advantage

Speed Is the Whole Game

A web designer takes six weeks.

Your co-founder builds the site in about five minutes.

Not a template. Their real services, their real reviews, their real hours, their photos, a contact form that works, and a chat assistant that answers their customers.

On the phone you won't say five minutes, because nobody believes it and it makes the work sound cheap.

"I can have a preview ready for you in 24 hours."

That one sentence is the strongest thing you'll say in this whole module. Nobody else in their inbox can say it, and it gives you a day of breathing room you probably won't need.

See it

This Is What You'll Hand Them

Three real sites, built exactly the way you are about to build yours.

Real reviews, real hours, real phone number. Every one of them started as a name on a list, exactly like the ones you're about to find.

Why the sale is easy

Nobody Pays You Until They Have Seen It

Think about how every other person who rings that business sells to them. They describe something. They send a quote for a thing that does not exist yet. They ask for money up front and a deposit for work nobody has seen.

You do the opposite.

The site is already built and on their screen before money is ever mentioned.

There is nothing to imagine and nothing to risk. They are not deciding whether to hire you. They are deciding whether to keep something that already exists with their name on it.

That is a completely different conversation, and it is why this works for someone who has never sold anything in their life.

Build first while you are learning. Later, once you are quick at it, you can turn it around and sell first. Blake explains when to make that switch.

The money

What an Afternoon Is Worth

What you hand over is a real single page site with their real photos, a working enquiry form, and a chat assistant that answers their customers.

The build takes minutes. The whole job is an afternoon.

To the owner it is worth real money, because they are comparing you to the designer who quoted them thousands two years ago and never got started.

$500 to $5,000 per client

The simple sites sit at the bottom of that range. The bigger ones, more pages and video, sit at the top. Landing the Client teaches you how to price it, so you do not have to work that out now.

Start on the simple ones on purpose. They are cheap and fast to build, so a mistake costs you a few cents and ten minutes instead of a real loss.

Three or four of those and something changes. You have real clients, real sites to show, and you have stopped guessing.

How you open the call

You Ring as a Customer, Not a Salesperson

Here is how a normal sales call goes. Someone rings, says they sell websites, and the owner switches off before the second sentence. They get those calls every week.

You do something different, and it takes ten seconds.

You ring the business the way a real customer would, trying to book them in, and you run into the exact problem you are calling about.

"I got you guys recommended by one of my neighbours. I tried to book you online, and I realised you don't have a website. Is there a reason for that?"

Now the owner is not listening to a pitch. They are listening to a customer who nearly went somewhere else.

They feel the problem for themselves, in their own words, before you have offered them anything.

The whole call is written out for you in Module 7, and you practise it with your co-founder before a real person ever hears you.

One niche

This Matters More Than It Sounds

A knee surgeon charges ten times what a GP does for the same hour.

Same years of training. One difference: they do one thing, so everyone trusts them with that thing.

Niching down works exactly the same here.

When you only serve plumbers, your fifth plumber call sounds like someone who has seen their exact mess before.

Generalists never get there.

And plumbers refer plumbers. One happy plumber talks to five others every week on job sites, and they all have the same problem.

So everything you build from here gets built around YOUR niche.

You picked it with your co-founder in Module 1. Every search, brief and site in this module assumes it.

Compounding

Every Site Sells the Next One

Every site you finish becomes a card on your own website: a screenshot, two sentences, a link.

After three sites, your calls change shape: "I built the sites for [two local businesses they know]. Want me to send you a link?"

From then on, the portfolio does the selling. You just dial.
