# Local Business Website Builder

The complete multi-step process for building a premium local business website inside Base44. This skill runs on a student's Superagent and handles everything from prospect intake to live preview.

## The goal

A student gives their agent a business name and (optionally) a website URL. The agent figures out the archetype, scrapes real assets, generates images if needed, builds the site, integrates real photos, polishes mobile, and hands back a live preview link. The process is broken into 6 prompts so students come back to their Superagent at each stage.

## How this skill works (for the student's agent)

The student drives this process. They paste prompts or give instructions at each step. Your job is to execute each step fully before reporting back and waiting for the student to move to the next step. Do NOT skip ahead or batch steps together unless the student explicitly asks.

The 6 steps are:
1. **Research this business**: student gives you a business name or Google Maps link, you scrape everything and identify the archetype
2. **Generate images**: if no real photos exist, student asks you to create AI images
3. **Create the app**: you tell the student what to name the app, they create it in Base44 manually
4. **Build the site**: you fire the full builder prompt with all research, images, and archetype design
5. **Review and fix**: student reviews the preview, you send fixes to the builder
6. **Deploy**: you walk the student through URL personalization or domain connection

## Step 1: Research this business

When a student says they want to build a website for a client, gather these in a quick conversation (one question at a time, friendly, not a form):

1. **Business name** (required)
2. **What they do** (required, one sentence: "plumber", "restaurant", "law firm", etc.)
3. **Their website URL** (if they have one, this is where we scrape assets from)
4. **Phone number** (for CTAs and tel: links)
5. **Location** (for service area and search)
6. **Anything specific the client wants** (particular sections, photos, branding)

If the student has the business's Google Maps listing, that's even better than a website for scraping real photos.

Once you have the basics, do the full research:

### Scrape their existing website (if they have one):
```bash
# Extract all image URLs from their homepage
curl -s "https://www.THEIRDOMAIN.com.au/" | grep -oP 'https?://[^"'"'"'\s]+\.(jpg|jpeg|png|webp|svg|mp4|webm)[^"'"'"'\s]*' | sort -u

# Also check common pages
curl -s "https://www.THEIRDOMAIN.com.au/about/" | grep -oP 'https?://[^"'"'"'\s]+\.(jpg|jpeg|png|webp)[^"'"'"'\s]*' | sort -u
curl -s "https://www.THEIRDOMAIN.com.au/gallery/" | grep -oP 'https?://[^"'"'"'\s]+\.(jpg|jpeg|png|webp)[^"'"'"'\s]*' | sort -u
curl -s "https://www.THEIRDOMAIN.com.au/projects/" | grep -oP 'https?://[^"'"'"'\s]+\.(jpg|jpeg|png|webp)[^"'"'"'\s]*' | sort -u
curl -s "https://www.THEIRDOMAIN.com.au/team/" | grep -oP 'https?://[^"'"'"'\s]+\.(jpg|jpeg|png|webp)[^"'"'"'\s]*' | sort -u

# Download the good ones (skip tiny icons, skip anything under 10KB)
curl -sL -o asset-name.jpg "IMAGE_URL"
```

### Also check Google Maps:
Search the business name on Google Maps. Download their posted photos. These are often the most authentic, real-world shots.

### Also check Facebook:
Their public photo albums often have job site photos, team candids, behind-the-scenes shots.

### What to grab (varies by archetype):
- **All archetypes:** Logo
- **Archetype A:** Work photos, team photos, van photos, project photos
- **Archetype B:** Food/product photos, interior photos, staff photos
- **Archetype C:** Professional headshots, office photos, credentials/awards
- **Archetype D:** Before/after photos (critical), action shots, van photos
- **Archetype E:** Inventory photos (the most important asset)

### Critical rule: never guess who's in a photo.
If you can't verify a photo is of a specific named person, do NOT use it for their card. Use a clean initial-avatar placeholder (circular avatar with their initial on a brand-colored background). Misattributing a photo erodes trust instantly.

### Check logo colors before designing:
The logo determines the header background color. Get this wrong and the logo is invisible.

```bash
python3 .agents/skills/local-biz-website-builder/scripts/check_logo_colors.py path/to/logo.png
```

This outputs the top solid colors and whether each is LIGHT (needs dark background) or DARK (needs light background).

### Then identify the archetype
Match the business to one of five archetypes (see Step 4 for full details). Report back to the student:
- What archetype the business is and why
- What business info you scraped (services, hours, reviews, contact)
- What images you found (or didn't)
- What's missing and what the student needs to get from the client
- Whether AI images are needed (Step 2)

Wait for the student to move to Step 2.

## Step 2: Generate images (only if no real photos)

If the business has zero photos (or only garbage quality), use the Base44 built-in image generator (`generate_image`) to create warm, on-brand placeholder images. Match the archetype's needs. Never use generic stock photos.

For a plumber with no photos, generate: a clean modern bathroom, a professional plumber at work, a service van. These should look intentional, not like free stock.

For a lawyer with no photos, generate: a professional office interior, a clean desk with legal books, a professional handshake or meeting scene.

For a lawn care business with no photos, generate: a manicured lawn, a person mowing, before/after lawn transformation.

Upload each generated image with `upload_file` to get a public URL.

Report back to the student with the image URLs and what each represents. Wait for them to move to Step 3.

If the business HAS good real photos, skip this step and go straight to Step 3.

## Step 4: Build the site

Fire the full builder prompt to the new app using `send_message_to_builder`. The prompt includes:
- All scraped business data (services, hours, reviews, contact)
- Image URLs (real scraped from Step 1 or AI-generated from Step 2)
- Archetype-specific design direction (see below)
- Chatbot configuration using invokeLLM
- Form entity creation for lead capture
- Mobile-first requirements

After sending the prompt, check with `get_base44_app_status` until the build is ready (usually 3-5 minutes).

### Universal builder prompt structure (all archetypes):

```
Build a premium, mobile-first single-page website for [BUSINESS NAME].

Design system:
- Warm, human, trustworthy. NOT corporate. NOT AI-gradient. Think premium local business.
- Font: [sans-serif system font or as appropriate]
- Colors: [PRIMARY COLOR from logo], [SECONDARY], [ACCENT]
- Header background: [based on logo color analysis - light logo needs dark bg, dark logo needs light bg]
- Generous whitespace, warm shadows, rounded corners (rounded-lg)
- Micro-interactions: hover scale, smooth transitions, entrance animations on scroll

Mobile requirements (critical):
- Every image in a fixed aspect-ratio container with object-fit: cover, never stretched
- No long stretches of text without a visual break (photo, icon, or textured background)
- Sticky bottom CTA bar: [CTA1] (filled) + [CTA2] (outline), always in thumb reach
- All phone numbers as tel: links
- Forms use type="tel" and type="email" for the right mobile keyboard
- Visual rhythm varies: alternate text-left/image-right, full-width photo bands, card grids
- No buttons overlapping the sticky bottom bar (remove redundant buttons on mobile)

Business details:
- Name: [BUSINESS NAME]
- Phone: [PHONE]
- Email: [EMAIL]
- Address: [ADDRESS]
- [LICENSE NUMBERS]
- Years in business: [YEARS]
- Google reviews: [COUNT]
- Service areas: [AREAS]

Chatbot:
- Create a backend function called [BusinessName]Chat that uses invokeLLM
- The bot answers questions about the business services, hours, and areas
- The bot NEVER invents prices. If asked about pricing, it says "Call [PHONE] for a quote" or "Book a consultation"
- The bot directs all booking/enquiry to the contact form or phone
- Add a chat widget to the bottom right of every page

Form:
- Create an entity called [EntityName] with fields matching the archetype form spec below
- The form on the page should save submissions to this entity
- Create a backend function to handle form submissions that creates a record in the entity
```

### Archetype A builder prompt (add to the universal structure):

**Archetype A: "Show Your Work" (trades)**
**Businesses:** plumbers, electricians, builders, roofers, landscapers, painters, tilers, aircon installers, fence builders
**Hero:** Full-bleed real photo of team/van/work site with warm gradient overlay. If they have a wide team or fleet shot, use it as the hero background. If only work photos exist, use the best one full-bleed.
**Sections (in order):** Hero, Trust badges, Services grid, How It Works (3 steps), Recent Work/Projects, Service Areas, Reviews, Team, FAQ, Final CTA + Contact form, Footer
**Imagery priority:** Work photos > team photos > van/equipment photos
**Trust signals:** License numbers, insurance, years in business, response time, Google review count
**Form:** Service request with: Service Type dropdown, Name, Phone, Email, Problem description, Urgency (standard/emergency)
**Mobile:** Sticky bottom bar: "Call Now" (filled) + "Book Online" (outline). All phone numbers as tel: links.
**Creative metaphor:** Often baked into the business name. "The Plumbing Doctor" = medical/diagnostic. Find it and weave through copy.
**Accent color:** Check logo. Trades often have yellow/amber/orange accents. Match the real brand color.

```

Centered content: small trust pill with review count and years, massive headline, subheadline, two CTAs (gold "Request Service" + outline "Call [PHONE]"), three trust pills at bottom (Upfront Pricing, Workmanship Guaranteed, Same Day Service). Scroll indicator.

SECTIONS:

```

### Archetype B builder prompt:

**Archetype B: "Show Your Space" (hospitality/retail)**
**Businesses:** restaurants, cafes, salons, gyms, bars, retail shops, bakeries, florists
**Hero:** Full-bleed photo of interior, food, or product. This IS the product. Minimal text overlay, let the image sell.
**Sections (in order):** Hero, Menu/Services with prices, Gallery, About (the story), Opening Hours (prominent), Location/Map, Reviews, Booking CTA + Form, Footer
**Imagery priority:** Product/food photos > interior photos > staff photos
**Trust signals:** Opening hours, price range, accessibility, parking, reviews, awards
**Form:** Booking/reservation with: Name, Phone, Email, Date, Time, Party size, Notes
**Mobile:** Sticky bottom bar: "Book Table" or "Book Now" (filled) + "Call" (outline). Hours and location instantly visible. "Get Directions" button.
**Special:** If they have a menu, build it as a structured menu section, not a PDF link.

```

SECTIONS:

2. Gallery: Masonry or grid of real photos (food, interior, ambiance). Hover zoom.

6. Reviews: Review cards with quotes, names, star ratings.
```

## Step 5: Upload and integrate real images

After the first build is ready (check with `get_base44_app_status`), upload each scraped image with `upload_file` to get a public URL, then send a follow-up builder message mapping each image to exactly where it goes:

```
Replace placeholder images with real photography:
- Hero background: [IMAGE_URL]
- About/Team photo: [IMAGE_URL]
- Team member 1 headshot: [IMAGE_URL]
- Team member 2 headshot: [IMAGE_URL]
- Project card 1: [IMAGE_URL]
- Project card 2: [IMAGE_URL]
- Logo: [IMAGE_URL]
- License badge: [IMAGE_URL]
```

Rules for image integration:
- Never stretch or squish images. Every image in a fixed aspect-ratio container with object-fit: cover.
- Use the widest, highest-quality photo as the hero background (full-bleed with gradient overlay).
- Team headshots should be square-cropped in fixed containers.
- Project photos should be landscape in fixed containers.
- If you can't verify who's in a photo, use an initial-avatar placeholder instead.

## Step 5b: Mobile review pass

Once images are integrated, do a mobile review:
1. Check the preview at 375px and 414px viewport widths (iPhone SE and iPhone Plus)
2. Look for: stretched images, buttons overlapping sticky bar, long text stretches with no visual break, sections that look identical stacked
3. Send a fix message to the builder addressing any issues found

Common fixes:
- "Remove the secondary outline Call button from the hero on mobile, the sticky bottom bar already has Call Now"
- "Add bottom padding to the page body on mobile equal to the sticky bar height so nothing gets clipped"
- "Add a photo or visual break to the [section name] section, it's a long stretch of text on mobile"
- "Fix image stretching in [section], use object-fit: cover in a fixed aspect-ratio container"

Report back to the student with the preview link. Ask them to check it on their phone. Wait for their feedback before sending fixes to the builder. 1-3 fix rounds is normal.

## Quick start (for the first simple builds)

If a student is just starting out, begin with Archetype A (trades). It's the simplest to build and the most common client type. The process:

1. Student gives you: business name, what they do, phone, area they cover, website URL (if any)
2. You scrape their site for images and identify the archetype (Step 1)
3. If no photos, you generate AI images (Step 2)
4. Student creates the app in Base44 (Step 3)
5. You fire the Archetype A builder prompt (Step 4)
6. You upload real images in a follow-up message (Step 5)
7. You do one mobile review pass (Step 5b)
8. You walk them through deployment (Step 6)

Total time: 15-30 minutes for a trades site. Longer if you need to wait for the business owner to provide photos.

## Chatbot details

The chatbot uses Base44's built-in `invokeLLM` integration. No external API keys needed.

The backend function should:
1. Accept a message from the chat widget
2. Pass it to invokeLLM with a system prompt that includes the business info (services, hours, phone, areas)
3. Return the reply to the chat widget

System prompt for the LLM should include:
- Business name and what they do
- Full list of services
- Business hours
- Phone numbers
- Service areas
- CRITICAL RULE: Never invent prices. If asked about pricing, say "Call [PHONE] for a quote" or "Book a consultation"
- Direct all booking/enquiry to the contact form or phone
- If the question is off-topic, politely redirect back to the business services
