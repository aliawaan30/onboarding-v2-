Module 2 · Build your business · Page A

Nobody buys from somebody who says they do a bit of everything

By the end of this module you will have a name, a look, a website that speaks straight to one kind of business, somewhere for enquiries to land, somewhere for the money to land, and a co-founder that knows every one of those details by heart.

Right now you can build the thing. What you cannot do yet is send somebody a link and have them believe you.

Page A: Pick your niche and name your business

Two things happen here. First you and your co-founder decide the one kind of business you are going to sell to, and the area you cover. Then you give your business a name, two colours, two fonts and a logo.

  1. 1

    Step 01Pick One Kind Of Business, In One Area

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    In the last module you picked what you sell. On this page you pick who you sell it to.

    One kind of business. Cafes. Or plumbers. Or dentists. Not a bit of everything.

    Here is why that matters. In a few minutes your co-founder writes the headline for your website. "Websites for cafes in Geelong" is a line a cafe owner stops on. "We build websites for businesses" is a line nobody stops on. Same goes for every email you send and every call you make after this.

    You also pick an area. Your own suburb or city, and roughly how far you would travel.

  2. 2

    Step 02Talk It Through With Your Co-Founder

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    It already knows where you live, what you did before this, and who you know. Let it use all of that.

    I'm picking one kind of business to go after first with my service. Use what you already know about me: where I'm based, what I did before this, who I already know, and what I said in the soul interview. Don't give me a list of ten. Ask me questions one at a time until you actually understand what I'd be comfortable ringing up. Two of those questions: have I ever worked in an industry myself, and does anyone I know run a business? If I've got a head start like that, use it, because a warm first call beats a cold one. Then give me your top 3, with a real reason for each one. For each one tell me: roughly how many of them are near me, whether they usually have money to spend, and how likely they are to already have what I'm selling.
    You should see: questions first, then three options, and the reasons mention real things about you. Your town, what you used to do, somebody you know.

    If the three options could have been handed to anybody, say that and ask it to go again.

  3. 3

    Step 03Lock In Your Niche And Your Area

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    I'm going after [YOUR PICK] first. Save it to memory as my_niche, permanently. From now on, when you write anything for my business, assume you're talking to this kind of business owner. Also save my service area as [YOUR SUBURB OR CITY, plus roughly how far you would travel]. Confirm both back to me in one line.
    You should see: your niche and your area read back to you in one line.

    Test it. In a brand new message ask "who am I selling to?" If it answers without a reminder, it stuck.

  4. 4

    Step 04Pick A Name That Works On The Phone

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    The name does not need to be clever. It needs three things.

    • Easy to say out loud when you ring somebody.
    • Easy to spell when they type it into their browser.
    • Still right in a year, when you might sell to more than one kind of business.

    You just picked one kind of business to start with. Your name should still work when you sell to a second kind. So do not name yourself after one suburb or one trade. Something like Springvale Plumber Websites is dead the day you sell to a cafe in the next town.

    You are not registering anything today. You are picking what goes on your website and your emails.

  5. 5

    Step 05Ask Your Co-Founder For Ten Name Options

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    I need a name for my agency. Use everything you already know about me: my service, my niche, where I'm based, and what I said in the soul interview about why I'm doing this. Give me 10 options in a plain list. For each one, one short line on why it fits me. Mix it up: some plain and clear, some with a bit of personality. Make all 10 genuinely different from each other. Don't give me ten versions of the same word with a different ending, and don't give me the names every agency already uses. Rules: easy to say out loud on a phone call, easy to spell after hearing it once, no made up words I'd have to spell out every time, and nothing that locks me to one suburb or one trade forever. Don't pick for me. I'll choose.
    You should see: ten names that are actually different from each other, and each reason mentions something real about you.

    If they all sound like the same name, ask for ten more and tell it what you did not like.

  6. 6

    Step 06Get A Straight Read On Your Top Three

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    I like these three: [PASTE YOUR THREE FAVOURITES]. For each one, tell me straight: how it sounds when I say "Hi, it's [my name] from [business name]" on a cold call, whether somebody could spell it after hearing it once, and anything about it that would annoy me in a year. Then give me your honest pick of the three and why.
  7. 7

    Step 07Say Your Favourite Out Loud

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    Say this sentence to yourself, properly out loud: "Hi, it's [your name] from [business name]."

    If you stumble on it, or you would have to spell it, pick a different one. You are going to say that line a few hundred times.

  8. 8

    Step 08Lock The Name In

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    My business name is [YOUR CHOICE]. Save it to memory as business_name, permanently. From now on, use it whenever you write anything for my business: my website, my emails, my proposals. Confirm it back to me in one line.
    You should see: one short line confirming it saved.

    Test it. In a brand new message ask "what's my business called?" If it answers without a reminder, it stuck.

  9. 9

    Step 09Get Three Options For Your Colours And Fonts

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    Nobody has ever bought from a local agency because of the logo. But a missing one makes you look like you started yesterday. Ten minutes and we move.

    Give me 3 options for how my business should look. For each option: two colours (give me the hex codes), one font for headings, one font for normal text, and one line on the feeling it gives off. Keep them simple and grown up. This has to look trustworthy to a local business owner who is about to hand me money, not like a tech startup.
    You should see: three full options, each with real codes like #1A3D5C, not just colour names.
  10. 10

    Step 10Lock The Look In

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    I'm going with option [1/2/3]. Save it to memory as brand_look: the two colour codes and the two fonts. Use it on everything you build for me from now on without me asking. Confirm it back in one line.
    You should see: your two colour codes and two fonts read back to you.
  11. 11

    Step 11Get Your Logo Made

    Finish the step above to unlock this one.

    Make me a logo image. It's a wordmark, which just means my business name written out in my heading font, in my brand colours, nothing else. No icons, no swooshes, no clip art. Plain background. Make it wide, the shape that sits in the top left corner of a website.
    You should see: an image of your business name in your colours.
    Read the words in the image carefully. AI gets letters wrong in images more often than you would think. If a single letter is mangled, that logo is unusable. Send this instead and move on:
    Don't generate the logo as an image. Instead, when you build my website, write my business name in the top left as plain text in my heading font and my brand colours. That's my logo for now.

    That is a completely normal way to run a business. Plenty of real agencies are a name in a nice font and nothing else.

Page complete

One kind of business, one area, and a name with a face on it.
