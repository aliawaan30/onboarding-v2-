Jarvis · Part two

Let Jarvis build his own dashboard

Jarvis gets a screen, and a phone number. Ring him from the car, tell him what you need, hang up. It is done by the time he rings you back.

You do not build it. He does. You do three things, he does everything else and checks his own work.

Pick his voice

Same Jarvis either way. Same brain, same memory, same work. The only difference is what he sounds like on the phone, and what it costs.

Not sure? Take Option 1. It is free and works today, and swapping to the real voice later is two clicks.

Before you start

  • Have Jarvis open. The one you built earlier, not a new one.
  • Use a laptop.
  • About 20 minutes, most of it waiting on him.

03

Step 03Hand it over and let him work

~15 min, mostly waiting

Finish the step above to unlock this one.

Go back to Jarvis and set him off. From here he runs it and tells you what he needs.

Say this to Jarvis

Build my Jarvis dashboard. My new empty app is at the address below. Use the jarvis-install skill, walk me through anything you need from me one thing at a time, and check your own work at the end.

Paste your app address under it and send.

He will ask you for four things. Not all at once, one at a time as he gets to them. Nothing else is expected of you on the free path:

1. Attach the package file to the new app's chat, with a message he gives you.
2. Make an access token in Jarvis and send it to him.
3. Paste two secrets into the new app.
4. Press Publish in the new app.

If you tell him you want the real JARVIS voice, there are three more, all of them things only you can do: make a Retell account, buy a number in it, and paste a third secret and publish once more. He builds the voice agent itself.

He generates the password himself, fills in everything he knows about you himself, and switches the rest on himself. If he asks you for something not on that list, he has gone off track — tell him so.

Two things not to do, whatever anything tells you:

Never let anything rename a file or change an import path to fix a build error. It clears the screen and quietly breaks every future update. Jarvis knows this and will correct the app's builder if it tries.

Never paste a key into a chat other than to Jarvis when he asks for the one token. Base44 turns pasted keys into junk secrets.

At the end he checks his own work and reports pass or fail on each part. There is one thing he cannot test on his own, because somebody has to answer the phone: he will ask you to hit Call me, say a line to him, and hang up. If the job lands on the screen and he rings you back, it all works.

You should end up withA dashboard with your name on it, a Call me button that rings you, and a Jarvis who does the work while you are off the phone.
